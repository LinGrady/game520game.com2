Dependency Review Checklist That Catches Real Product Risk

By James ChenPublished June 18, 202610 min read

Dependencies are product decisions disguised as engineering convenience. A package, API, vendor SDK, or internal platform can save months of work. It can also introduce security exposure, licensing problems, operational coupling, upgrade burden, and failure modes the team does not control.

Review the job before the tool

Start by naming the job the dependency performs. Is it authentication, payments, chart rendering, logging, model inference, file processing, or data sync? A dependency that handles sensitive data or critical workflow deserves more review than a small formatting helper.

Check ownership and maintenance

Who maintains it? How often is it updated? Are security issues handled publicly and quickly? Is there a bus factor problem? Open-source popularity helps, but it is not enough. A widely used package can still be undermaintained.

Understand failure behavior

What happens when the dependency is slow, unavailable, rate-limited, or returns bad data? Teams often review happy-path integration and skip degraded behavior. A good review includes timeout settings, retries, circuit breakers, fallbacks, and customer-facing impact.

Look at data and permissions

Does the dependency process personal data, secrets, payments, files, prompts, or customer content? Does it require broad permissions? Can access be scoped? Can logs leak sensitive data? These questions matter before production, not after an audit.

Plan the exit

Every important dependency should have an exit story. That does not mean building a full abstraction for everything. It means knowing where coupling lives, what data would need migration, and how painful replacement would be if pricing, reliability, or trust changed.

A short checklist

  • What product job does this dependency perform?
  • What data does it touch?
  • Who maintains it and how are vulnerabilities handled?
  • What happens when it fails?
  • How often will we need to upgrade it?
  • How hard would replacement be?

The goal of dependency review is not to say no to every external tool. The goal is to make coupling intentional.
