Building Your First AI Product: A Step-by-Step Guide from Concept to Launch

👤 Richard Chen 📅 March 19, 2026 ⏱️ 20 min read
Advertisement

Eight weeks ago, we had nothing but an idea: a tool that helps small businesses write better customer support responses using AI. Today, that tool is live with 47 paying customers and $3,200 in monthly recurring revenue.

This wasn't our first AI project, but it was our first time building an AI product from scratch as a small team. In this guide, I'll share everything we learned—the technical decisions, the team structure, the mistakes we made, and the go-to-market strategy that actually worked.

Week 1-2: Research and Planning

Before writing a single line of code, we spent two weeks answering three critical questions:

1. What Problem Are We Solving?

We interviewed 23 small business owners who handled their own customer support. The pain point was clear: writing good responses took too long, especially for complex issues.

One quote that stuck with us: "I spend 3 hours every day answering the same types of emails. I know what to say, I just don't have time to write it out nicely."

2. Is There a Market?

We validated demand through:

  • A waitlist landing page (got 340 signups in 2 weeks)
  • Direct interviews with potential customers
  • Analysis of competitor pricing and customer reviews

3. Can We Build It?

As former Google engineers, we had the technical skills. But we needed to be realistic about what we could build in 8 weeks with just two people.

Week 3-5: Development

Technical Architecture

We chose a simple architecture that we could iterate on quickly:

  • Frontend: Next.js with TypeScript (fast to build, good developer experience)
  • Backend: Python FastAPI (great for AI/ML integration)
  • AI Model: OpenAI API (GPT-4 for quality, GPT-3.5-turbo for cost optimization)
  • Database: PostgreSQL via Supabase (easy to set up, good free tier)
  • Hosting: Vercel (frontend) + Railway (backend)

Code Example: Our AI Response Generator

Here's the core function that generates customer support responses:


def generate_response(customer_email: str, tone: str = "professional") -> str:
    """
    Generate a customer support response based on the incoming email.
    """
    prompt = f"""You are a helpful customer support representative. 
    Write a response to the following customer email.
    
    Customer Email:
    {customer_email}
    
    Tone: {tone}
    
    Guidelines:
    - Acknowledge their concern
    - Provide specific solutions
    - Keep it concise (under 150 words)
    - End with a helpful closing
    
    Response:"""

    response = openai.ChatCompletion.create(
        model="gpt-3.5-turbo",
        messages=[
            {"role": "system", "content": "You are a helpful customer support representative."},
            {"role": "user", "content": prompt}
        ],
        temperature=0.7,
        max_tokens=300
    )
    
    return response.choices[0].message.content

Cost Management Strategy

One of our biggest concerns was AI costs. At scale, AI API calls could eat up all our revenue. Here's how we manage costs:

  • Use GPT-3.5-turbo for 90% of requests (10x cheaper than GPT-4)
  • Only escalate to GPT-4 for complex or sensitive queries
  • Cache common responses to reduce API calls
  • Implemented request limits per user (free: 10/day, paid: 100/day)

Week 6-7: Testing and Beta

Beta Program

We launched a beta with 50 users from our waitlist. Key learnings:

  • Users wanted more control over the output tone
  • The interface needed to show the "before" and "after" clearly
  • 50% of users wanted to save and reuse good responses
  • Response time mattered more than we thought

Iterating Based on Feedback

We made three major pivots based on beta feedback:

  1. Added tone selector: Professional, Friendly, Empathetic
  2. Built response history: Users can save and edit past responses
  3. Optimized for speed: Added response caching to reduce latency from 3s to 0.8s

Week 8: Launch

Launch Strategy

We didn't do a big public launch. Instead, we focused on:

  • Email to waitlist: Our 340 waitlist signups became our first customers
  • Product Hunt: Got featured and got 180 upvotes (brought 23 customers)
  • Reddit r/smallbusiness: Shared our story and got genuine interest
  • Twitter/X: Documented our journey, built a following of 400 people

Pricing That Works

We tested three pricing tiers:

  • Free: 10 responses/day (converts ~5% to paid)
  • Pro ($19/month): 100 responses/day + history (60% choose this)
  • Business ($49/month): Unlimited + team features (35% choose this)

What We Would Do Differently

Mistake 1: Started with GPT-4

Our initial version used GPT-4 for everything. It worked great but cost us $0.08 per response. When we switched to GPT-3.5-turbo for most requests, our costs dropped to $0.002 per response—a 40x reduction.

Mistake 2: Built Features Nobody Asked For

We spent a week building a "sentiment analysis" feature that beta users never used. Now we validate every feature with at least 5 potential users before building.

Mistake 3: Delayed Launch

We wanted the product to be "perfect" before launching. In retrospect, we should have launched earlier with less functionality. The feedback from real users was more valuable than any internal testing.

Key Takeaways

  1. Validate first: Talk to potential customers before building anything
  2. Start simple: Build the minimum viable product and iterate based on feedback
  3. Manage costs from day one: AI API costs can quickly spiral out of control
  4. Launch early: Perfect is the enemy of good. Get something out there.
  5. Listen to users: Your customers will tell you what they actually want

What's Next

We're now at $3,200 MRR and growing 15% month-over-month. Our next priorities are:

  • Adding multi-language support (requested by 40% of customers)
  • Building an API for power users
  • Exploring smaller, fine-tuned models for specific use cases
