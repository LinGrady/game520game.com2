Cloud Bill Review Routine

By James ChenPublished June 22, 20269 min read

Cloud cost reviews fail when they become a monthly argument over a spreadsheet nobody trusts. A better routine connects spending changes to architecture decisions while the team still remembers why the change happened.

Review deltas, not only totals

The total bill matters, but the useful conversation starts with changes: which service increased, which environment grew, which data transfer pattern appeared, and whether the change maps to a planned release.

Ask for owner and reason

Every meaningful cost increase needs an owner and a reason. "Storage increased" is not enough. "Search logs retained for 90 days after incident review" is a decision the team can evaluate.

Separate waste from investment

Some increases are healthy: more customers, better observability, safer backups. Others are waste: forgotten test clusters, oversized instances, unused snapshots. Treating all cost as bad makes teams hide useful spending.

Close with one action

A review should produce a small number of actions: delete unused resources, add budget alerts, change retention, resize a workload, or document why a cost is accepted. Ten vague actions usually mean nothing happens.
