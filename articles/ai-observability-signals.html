AI Observability Signals That Matter After Launch

By James ChenPublished June 18, 202610 min read

AI features need observability beyond uptime and latency. A model endpoint can be fast and available while giving low-quality answers, retrieving the wrong documents, costing too much, or failing silently for a subset of users. Traditional service metrics are necessary, but they are not enough.

Track input and output shape

Record request type, prompt size, response size, refusal rate, tool calls, and structured output validity. You do not need to store sensitive content to learn whether the system is drifting toward longer prompts, malformed outputs, or unexpected usage patterns.

Measure retrieval quality

For RAG systems, watch empty retrievals, low-confidence retrievals, repeated documents, stale documents, and user follow-up patterns. If users keep asking the same question again, the first answer may not have helped.

Monitor cost per successful task

Token cost alone is not the right metric. A cheap answer that fails the task is expensive. Track estimated cost per completed workflow, not just cost per call. This helps teams choose caching, model routing, prompt trimming, and fallback strategies intelligently.

Build feedback loops

Thumbs-up buttons are not enough. Capture correction edits, escalations to humans, regenerated answers, abandoned flows, and support tickets tied to AI behavior. These signals reveal quality problems that raw model metrics miss.

Separate safety from satisfaction

A safe answer can still be useless. A satisfying answer can still violate policy. Treat safety checks, factuality checks, task success, and user satisfaction as separate dimensions. Combining them into one score hides the trade-offs teams need to manage.

AI observability is the practice of making product quality visible after launch. Without it, teams are flying with green infrastructure dashboards and no idea whether the feature is actually helping.
